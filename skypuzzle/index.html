How to play

Each piece is a real ALMA image of a gravitationally lensed galaxy, blown up 1300× so you can see it — and it starts badly stretched, so slide it brighter until the ring or arc appears.

Every one of them sits on a source in the Herschel/SPIRE maps below — but Herschel's beam is far too coarse to tell them apart, so the board shows each unfound galaxy as a faint preview at its true spot. Match the shape, drag the piece there, and it clicks into place.

You start with the stand-outs: 82 curated pieces, the ones with a ring, an arc or multiple images worth looking at. The button in the Options menu (or the A key) swaps them for all 297 ALMA snapshots of the survey — the same puzzle at full length, most of the extra pieces faint blobs rather than showpieces. Whatever you have already placed stays placed when you switch.

Streak counts how many you place in a row: every piece that clicks home adds one, and any drop that does not lands you back at zero. Your best run is kept.

Coordinates draws the RA/Dec grid and puts each piece's sky position in its panel — the astronomer's way to find it. Positions just rings the spots that are still empty. Scroll or pinch to zoom; drag empty sky to pan.

Whole sky (or the V key) swaps the packed board for the real celestial sphere: the same six fields at their true right ascension and declination, over the Milky Way, the constellations and every star down to naked-eye magnitude 6. It is worth a look — the fields hug the galactic poles because that is where the Galaxy's own dust is thinnest, and Fomalhaut sits inside the SGP field.
